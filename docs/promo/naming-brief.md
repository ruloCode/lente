# Naming brief: a commercial name for Lente with global reach

For the people (or AI research agents) who will research and brainstorm the product's commercial name. Read the whole brief before you start; everything you need is here or linked.

Owner: Rulo (sole author of Lente). Brief written 2026-10-29.

## 1. The decision we need

Lente is the working name. We want to know which name will give the product the most impact **worldwide**, and whether that name is "Lente", "Dilo", or something new.

Your job is to bring back a **ranked shortlist of 5 names** with evidence, and a clear recommendation: keep "Lente", change now, or change after the hackathon. You do not choose the name. The owner does.

**Deadline:** the final report by **2026-11-01, 23:59 Bogotá time** (UTC−5). The hackathon submission closes on 2026-11-04, 9:00 PM ET, and the demo video, landing page and app icon need the final name before then. A preliminary top 15 by **2026-10-31, 12:00 Bogotá** lets the owner react early.

## 2. The product in plain words

Lente is a real-time meeting copilot for people who work in their second language.

- A small clip on **any pair of glasses** (camera + microphone) listens to the meeting and sees what the wearer sees. It also works from the phone or the laptop, with no clip.
- When someone asks the wearer a question, the phone shows **one line they can say right away**, 90 characters or less. That card is called **"Dilo"** (Spanish for "say it"). Below the line, its meaning in the wearer's language.
- It also explains a term the wearer missed, says what is on the slide in front of them, summarizes the meeting, keeps the day as text (never audio), and prepares the follow-up after the meeting.
- It runs on Meta's AI models and was built for the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon (Oct–Nov 2026).

The problem it solves: in a live meeting in a second language the hard part isn't vocabulary, it's **speed**. By the time you've translated in your head, the moment has passed, and you stay quiet in the meetings that decide your work.

More: [README](../../README.md), [architecture](../architecture.md), promo images in this folder.

## 3. Who the name must work for

- **Primary users:** engineers, designers, founders and other professionals who work in English as a second language. First market: Latin America (Spanish and Portuguese speakers). Then every large non-native-English workforce: India, Brazil, Mexico, Southeast Asia, Eastern Europe, China, Japan, Korea, the Middle East, Western Europe.
- **The meeting's other people:** they will see the clip and hear the name. The name must sound respectable in a work meeting, not like a toy or a cheat tool.
- **Judges, press, investors:** mostly English speakers in tech. The name must be easy to say in a 3-minute video and easy to type after hearing it once.

## 4. Brand position (the name must fit this)

From the brand study of 2026-10-28 ([decisions](../kb/decisions.md), "Brand Calibre, card Dilo"):

- Competitors (Even Realities, Cluely, Omi, Brilliant Labs, Mentra) are light, gadget-store or fashion styled. **Cluely sells being undetectable.**
- Lente takes the opposite ground: **a calm, honest instrument** that asks permission, shows a visible listening light, and shows its measured numbers. Visual identity "Calibre": dark graphite, cyan only for the line to say, amber only for measured numbers, a lens-and-line logo.
- Core promise in one sentence: *you get your voice back in meetings held in your second language.*

So the name must suggest **clarity, confidence, voice, being present**. It must **not** suggest spying, hiding, cheating, whispering in secret, or "AI talks for you".

## 5. Hard constraints (a name that fails one is out)

1. **No third-party marks** in the name or close to them: Meta, Muse, Ray-Ban, Oakley, Llama, Glass, Spectacles, Vision Pro, Copilot, Otter, and the competitor names above.
2. **No negative, vulgar, ridiculous or sensitive meaning** in any language of the check list (section 7.3), including slang, and no unfortunate sound-alikes.
3. **No identical or confusingly similar registered trademark** in Nice classes **9** (devices, software) or **42** (SaaS, AI services) in the US, EU or Latin America's large markets (section 7.4).
4. **Not covert or deceptive** in tone (see section 4).
5. **Not locked to glasses.** The product already runs from the phone and the laptop and will add memory and an assistant. A name that only means "eyewear" limits it.

## 6. What a strong name looks like (the scoring rubric)

Score each surviving candidate 1–5 on each criterion; multiply by the weight; the maximum is 500.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Global pronounceability and spelling | 20 | Said the same way by speakers of all check-list languages; spelled correctly after hearing it once |
| Fit with the promise | 20 | Evokes voice, clarity, confidence or presence without explaining the feature literally |
| Distinctiveness and trademark strength | 20 | Invented or arbitrary in the category; no close marks in classes 9/42; not a generic word |
| Digital availability | 15 | A strong domain (.com, or .ai / .app with the .com not used by a competitor), matching handles on the main platforms |
| Memorability and brevity | 10 | 1–3 syllables, 4–8 letters, sticks after one hearing |
| Room to grow | 10 | Works for the clip, the phone app, desktop mode and future products |
| Fit with "Calibre" and "Dilo" | 5 | Sits well next to the "Dilo" card and the calm, dark instrument look |

"Dilo" stays the name of the card action unless you recommend promoting it to the product name. Say how each finalist pairs with it (e.g. "Open X, tap Dilo").

## 7. How to do it

### 7.1 Research the landscape (about 20% of the time)

Look up and note how these products are named and positioned. For each: name, what the name means or evokes, tagline, domain, one line on why it works or not.

- Smart glasses and wearables: Even Realities, Brilliant Labs (Frame, Halo), Mentra, Meta Ray-Ban Display, Xreal, Rokid, Omi, Bee, Limitless, Plaud, Humane, Rabbit.
- Meeting and language tools: Cluely, Otter, Granola, Fireflies, Fathom, tl;dv, Krisp, Read AI, DeepL Voice, Google Meet live translation, Interprefy, Wordly.
- Language-confidence and speaking apps: Duolingo, Praktika, Speak, ELSA, Langotalk, and others you find.

Then answer: which naming patterns are saturated in this category (e.g. "-ly", "AI" suffix, "Lens/Glass/Vision", "Copilot") and which territories are still open?

### 7.2 Brainstorm (about 30%)

Generate **at least 150 raw candidates** before you judge any of them. Work through these territories and at least three techniques per territory. Don't stop at English: use Latin, Spanish, Portuguese, Greek, Sanskrit, Japanese, Swahili, Esperanto roots, and invented words.

Territories:
- **Voice and speaking:** saying, answering, your turn, the floor, speaking up.
- **Clarity and sight:** lens, focus, seeing clearly, light (the current name sits here).
- **Confidence and presence:** being there, holding your ground, poise, courage.
- **Timing and speed:** the moment, the cue, right now, the beat before you speak.
- **Bridge and fluency:** crossing between languages without "translation" clichés.
- **Instrument and craft:** calibration, measurement, a trusted tool (the "Calibre" identity).
- **Invented / abstract:** short coined words with no meaning that sound good everywhere (like Kodak, Xerox, Zoom-style simplicity).

Techniques: compounds, blends (portmanteau), truncations, letter swaps, foreign roots, metaphors, real words used arbitrarily, two-word names, names with an action verb.

Also include, as candidates to be scored on equal footing: **Lente**, **Dilo**, and one or two variants of each.

### 7.3 Screen for language and culture (about 20%)

Cut the list to about **40** using the hard constraints and a quick rubric pass. For those 40:

- **Meaning check** in at least these languages: English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Mandarin, Cantonese, Hindi, Arabic, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Indonesian/Malay, Turkish, Vietnamese, Tagalog, Polish. Check dictionaries, slang sources (e.g. Urban Dictionary and local equivalents) and, where possible, a native speaker.
- **Say-it test:** say the name out loud (or record it) and ask people from at least five language backgrounds to write down what they heard and to say it back. Record misspellings and mispronunciations.
- **Search collision:** search the name on Google, the App Store and Google Play. Note what owns page one. A name buried under a large existing brand or a common word is a weak name for discovery.

### 7.4 Check availability (about 20%)

For the top **15**:

- **Domains:** .com, .ai, .app, .co, and one country domain if relevant. Use an RDAP or WHOIS lookup, not only a registrar's "available" banner. If taken, note whether it is parked, for sale (and asking price if listed) or an active business.
- **Handles:** X, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn (company page), YouTube, GitHub.
- **Trademarks,** classes 9 and 42 (note 38 and 41 if you find hits): USPTO Trademark Search (US), EUIPO eSearch / TMview (EU), WIPO Global Brand Database (international), SIC (Colombia), IMPI (Mexico), INPI (Brazil). Record exact and similar marks, their status (live, dead) and classes.
- **App stores:** apps with the same or a close name.

This is a screen, not legal clearance. Say so in the report; a trademark attorney checks the final pick before any filing.

### 7.5 Shortlist and test (about 10%)

Pick **5 finalists**. For each, run a small test with at least 8 people, ideally professionals who work in English as a second language, from at least 4 countries:

1. Show the name for 5 seconds, then ask an hour later (or at the end of the session) which names they remember.
2. Ask what kind of product they imagine from the name alone.
3. Ask them to pick the one they would trust in a work meeting.

Small numbers are fine; report the raw counts, not percentages dressed up as statistics.

## 8. What to deliver

One Markdown report (e.g. `naming-report.md`) plus one spreadsheet or CSV. Send them to the owner; don't commit them yourself.

**The report:**

1. **Recommendation** (half a page): the name you would pick, the runner-up, and keep / change now / change after the hackathon, with the reason. Weigh the cost of a change before 2026-11-04: repo name, firmware, phone app, docs, video.
2. **Landscape summary:** the naming patterns found and the open territory (1 page max).
3. **The 5 finalists**, one section each:
   - Name, pronunciation (IPA and a plain spelling), meaning or origin.
   - Rubric score with a one-line justification per criterion.
   - Language check results and any risks found.
   - Availability with links and the **date checked**: domains, handles, trademark hits.
   - Test results (raw counts).
   - A suggested tagline, and how it pairs with "Dilo".
4. **"Lente" and "Dilo" scored** on the same rubric, even if neither is a finalist, so the owner can compare.
5. **Rejected names worth knowing** (10–20): why each fell out.

**The spreadsheet:** every raw candidate (150+), its territory, the stage where it was cut and why, and the scores for those that got scored.

## 9. Rules

- **Cite everything.** Every availability or trademark claim has a link and the date checked. Anything you could not verify is marked "unverified".
- **Don't buy, register or reserve anything** (domains, handles, trademarks) without the owner's written OK. Domain searches on some registrars can trigger front-running; prefer RDAP/WHOIS lookups, and don't type the finalists into a registrar's search box more than needed.
- **Keep the shortlist private** until the owner decides. Don't post candidates on social media, forums or public AI-sharing links.
- **Don't copy** another brand's name, tagline or logo, and don't "borrow" a competitor's naming idea with a letter changed.
- The product description in this brief is public (the repository is public). Nothing else about the owner, the meetings or the users is to be shared.
- No real meeting audio, transcripts or photos of people are needed for this work; don't use any.

## 10. If you are an AI research agent

- Split the work by phase; the brainstorm (7.2) must finish before scoring starts, so early ideas don't anchor the list.
- Run language checks per language in parallel, and say for each which sources you used. A model's own sense of a word's meaning in a language is not a source; flag those results as "model judgment, needs a native speaker".
- Do the say-it test (7.3) and the user test (7.5) only with real people; if you can't, write "not done" and leave those cells empty. Never simulate test participants.
- Stop and ask the owner if a hard constraint is ambiguous for a strong candidate, rather than dropping or keeping it silently.
